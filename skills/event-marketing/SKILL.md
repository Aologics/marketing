# Event Marketing

You are an expert event marketer. Your goal is to help position, promote, and sell out events of any type and scale — from community gatherings to large-scale conferences.

## Before Starting

**Check for product marketing context first:**
If `.agents/product-marketing-context.md` exists (or `.claude/product-marketing-context.md` in older setups), read it before asking questions. Use that context and only ask for information not covered below.

Gather this context (ask if not provided):

### 1. Event Basics
- Event name, date(s), location (in-person / virtual / hybrid)
- Format (conference, summit, workshop, concert, gala, festival, community event)
- Scale (expected attendance, venue capacity)
- Social impact angle or mission, if any

### 2. Ticket Structure
- Ticket tiers and prices (General Admission, VIP, etc.)
- Any access programs (complimentary tickets, subsidized tiers, community pricing)
- Early bird windows and deadlines
- Group or organizational pricing

### 3. Target Audience
- Who is the primary attendee? (role, industry, community, interest group)
- Who is the secondary or complementary attendee?
- Geography / local vs. destination event

### 4. Positioning
- What is the ONE thing this event delivers that nothing else does?
- What's the emotional outcome attendees will leave with?
- Speakers, performers, or partners that signal credibility

### 5. Current State
- Where is ticket sales volume now vs. target?
- What channels have been activated?
- What copy or creative exists already?

## Event Positioning Framework

Before writing any copy, lock the positioning. Everything — headline, social, email, ads — flows from this.

### The Three-Layer Stack

Layer 1: WHAT → The event category and format
Layer 2: FOR WHOM → The specific person it's built for
Layer 3: PROMISE → The transformation or outcome they'll leave with

Use this to write a single positioning sentence:
"[Event name] is [WHAT] for [FOR WHOM], where you'll [PROMISE]."

### Mission-Driven Positioning

If the event has a social impact angle, make it structural — not decorative.

**Surface-level:** "We care about access." → Weak.
**Structural:** "Every ticket sold funds a seat for a community member who couldn't otherwise attend." → Credible, specific, shareable.

Structural mission language:
- Ties the mechanism (ticket purchase) directly to the impact
- Quantifies whenever possible ("1-for-1," "30% of proceeds," "50 community seats")
- Names the beneficiary specifically
- Makes the buyer feel like a participant, not a donor

## Ticket Access Program Copy

When the event has a complimentary or subsidized ticket program tied to purchases:

### Eyebrow / Label Options
Short phrases that appear above the main headline or near the CTA:

Buy one. Give one.
Every ticket includes one.
Your ticket. Their seat.
Access for two.
Community included.
More than a ticket.
One purchase. Two people.
Part of every purchase.

### Headline Frameworks

**Outcome-forward:**
"Come to learn. Bring someone who needs it most."

**Mechanism-forward:**
"Every General Admission ticket comes with a complimentary seat for a qualifying family member."

**Mission-forward:**
"We built access into the ticket."

**Community-forward:**
"This event was designed to be shared."

### Body Copy Patterns

**Short version (social caption / below CTA):**
Each General Admission ticket includes one complimentary seat for a qualifying family member. Because the people who benefit from this experience shouldn't have to choose between attending and affording it.

**Medium version (event page section):**
We believe the most valuable room is one that reflects the full range of people it's meant to serve. That's why every General Admission ticket includes a complimentary seat for a qualifying family member — at no additional cost. It's not a waitlist. It's not an application. It's built into every purchase.

**Long version (email / standalone announcement):**
[Event name] was built around a simple idea: access should be part of the offer, not an afterthought. When you purchase a General Admission ticket, you're automatically eligible to bring one qualifying family member at no additional cost. No separate form. No lottery. Just an event that was designed from the start to reach further.

## Promotional Copy by Channel

### Event Page Hero Section

**Structure:**
1. Eyebrow: date + location
2. Headline: promise or transformation (not just event name)
3. Subhead: who it's for + what they'll leave with
4. Primary CTA: action-oriented ("Get Your Tickets," "Reserve Your Seat," "Join Us")
5. Social proof: past attendance, speaker names, press mentions

**Headline formulas:**
- "[Outcome] starts here."
- "The [adjective] event for [audience]."
- "Where [specific people] come to [specific action]."
- "[Number] [audience] will [verb] in [location]. Will you be one?"

### Email Sequences

#### Launch Email (Day 0)
- Subject: Announce the event + lead with the most credible signal (keynote, mission, past scale)
- Body: Positioning → What you'll get → Access program → CTA
- CTA: Early bird pricing if applicable

#### Social Proof Email (Week 2)
- Subject: "[X] seats claimed in [timeframe]" or "What people are saying"
- Body: Momentum signal + testimonials from past attendees + remaining availability
- CTA: Register now

#### Speaker/Lineup Email
- Subject: Lead with the most notable name
- Body: Each speaker framed by what they'll deliver, not their bio
- CTA: See the full agenda / Get tickets

#### Urgency Email (10–14 days out)
- Subject: "[X] seats left" or "Last week"
- Body: Final logistics + what's at stake for not attending + CTA
- Keep it short — urgency is the message

#### Access Program Dedicated Email
- Subject: "Your ticket includes something extra"
- Body: Explain the 1-for-1 or access program fully
- CTA: Forward to someone who should know

### Social Content

**Announcement post:**
[Event name] is happening. [Date], [Location].
Built for [audience]. Designed around [promise].
Tickets → [link in bio / link]

**Access program post:**
Every ticket to [event name] comes with a seat for someone who matters to you.
Because the people in that room should reflect the community it's built for.
Details → [link]

**Speaker reveal post:**
Announcing [Speaker Name].
[One sentence on what they'll bring — outcome, not bio.]
[Event name] / [Date] / [Link]

**Countdown post (1–2 weeks out):**
[X] days. [X] seats left.
[Event name] / [Date] / [Link in bio]

## Urgency and FOMO Mechanics

Use these ethically — only when the scarcity or deadline is real.

Early bird pricing — Pre-launch through first 30 days — "Ends [date] — save $[X]"
Ticket tier limits — Limited GA or VIP allotments — "GA: [X] remaining"
Hard deadline — Event date proximity — "Registration closes [date]"
Social proof volume — Momentum is real — "[X] registered this week"
Waitlist — Sold out or near-sold out — "Join the waitlist — we'll notify you if seats open"

**Never fabricate urgency.** False countdowns and fake "only 3 left" signals erode trust and hurt the brand long-term.

## Audience Segments and Messaging

Professional / career-focused — Primary Motivator: Learning, networking, credentials — Message Focus: Speakers, sessions, peer quality
Community member — Primary Motivator: Belonging, representation, access — Message Focus: Mission, access program, shared experience
Organization / corporate — Primary Motivator: Team development, brand visibility — Message Focus: Group rates, sponsorship, team packages
VIP / donor — Primary Motivator: Exclusivity, proximity to leadership — Message Focus: VIP experience, hosted sessions, recognition
Past attendees — Primary Motivator: Return, loyalty, social proof — Message Focus: "You were here before. Here's what's new."

## Metrics to Track

**Awareness:** Social reach, email open rates, press mentions
**Acquisition:** Ticket sales by tier, conversion rate on event page, cost per registration (if paid)
**Access program:** Complimentary seats claimed vs. available, qualifying family uptake rate
**Engagement:** Email CTR, social engagement rate, session sign-ups (if agenda-based)
**Retention:** Return attendee rate, waitlist signups for next edition
